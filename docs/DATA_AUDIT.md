# Data Audit - what the provided files really contain

Generated by `scripts/audit_report.py` from `taptrace/catalog.py` and `taptrace/siis.py`. These findings drive the design.

## 1. deeplinks.json (Settings catalog)

* Declared count 578, rows 578, usable phone rows after quarantine **549**, distinct screens **397**, screens with several control variants **133**.
* URI scheme in the data is `voiceassist://masked/act/...` + `voiceassist://masked/val/...` (the PDF says `bixby://`); the only placeholder is `voiceassist://dummy_positive`.
* **TV distractors (18)**: DL-0486, DL-0488, DL-0489, DL-0497, DL-0498, DL-0502, DL-0507, DL-0508, DL-0511, DL-0512, DL-0531, DL-0532, DL-0540, DL-0562, DL-0566, DL-0567, DL-0568, DL-0569

* Ids run row_1..row_22 with **row_6 and row_18 absent**; 20 records, 11 unique documents.
* Link/e-mail patterns found by the zero-leak regex inside 'pre-cleaned' SIIS text: 3 (e.g. an e-mail address in the Kids-PIN section), plus 'check out our website' / 'provided links' phrasing.
* Every content field starts with a product-category prefix (`Smartphone,Others Mobile,... Title ( ... ):`) that is stripped.
* Several documents are the wrong document for their complaint (Multi-window for a dark tablet or a floating menu; TV mirroring for a small phone screen; camera-video flicker for a Fold that flickers when opened) - handled by the Relevance Gate.
* All 20 complaints are Display-domain although the PDF names Battery / Camera / Performance too - the mapping benchmark therefore covers all domains (`eval/mapping_benchmark.json`).
